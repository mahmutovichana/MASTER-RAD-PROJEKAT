# Synthetic Dataset Risk Assessment 2026-08

## Summary

The v0.4 synthetic DocGuard dataset is valuable, but it should be treated as a controlled prototype benchmark rather than the sole final evaluation dataset.

## Main Risks

- Generator/template bias: records are generated by project code, so models may learn generator regularities rather than general documentation-update reasoning.
- Regular scenario names: labels such as `changed_validation_min`, `added_response_field`, and `internal_variable_rename_no_behavior_change` are clean and systematic.
- Repeated templates: even with project-level splits and hash checks, code and documentation patterns may remain structurally similar across splits.
- Unrealistic patches: generated documentation patches are useful for tests, but may be simpler than real Markdown documentation changes.
- Synthetic negatives: no-update examples may not represent the messy variety of real developer behavior.
- Clean metrics may overestimate real performance: perfect or near-perfect results on v0.4 do not prove real-world generalization.
- `full_current` leakage: summaries and extracted signals can encode label semantics close to the gold labels.
- Negative subtype labels: these are diagnostic and may be more artificial than binary no-update labels.

## What the Synthetic Dataset Is Still Good For

- Controlled development of the pipeline.
- Debugging validators, routers, metrics, and UI.
- VS Code demo and runtime smoke tests.
- Sanity-checking ML/HF/Hybrid components.
- Ablation experiments with known scenario coverage.
- Demonstrating the end-to-end concept.

## Recommended Framing

Use synthetic v0.4 as a controlled prototype benchmark. Do not present it as the only final evidence. The thesis should add an external real-world validation benchmark and report synthetic and real results separately.
